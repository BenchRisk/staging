## The mitigation under review

- **Mitigation number:** 58
- **Mitigates failure mode:** #32 — *"Evaluator(s) have a high false reject rate
  (acceptable outputs rated as unacceptable)"*
- **Affected reliability dimension:** Correctness
- **Benchmark production stage:** (4) Output Evaluation
- **Risk reduction if adopted:** ~26.67% reduction in failure-mode likelihood; ~58.33%
  reduction in severity (context only — do not let these numbers change your verdict).
- **Example realization of the failure mode:** A benchmark uses human evaluators who are
  instructed to flag any potential safety concerns in SUT responses to medical questions.
  The evaluators, anxious about missing harmful content and lacking medical expertise,
  excessively reject valid medical information that mentions side effects or treatment
  risks, even when accurately presented with appropriate context. As a result, systems
  that provide thorough, nuanced medical information score poorly compared to those giving
  oversimplified but incomplete advice. The benchmark user selects a SUT that omits
  important risk information in its responses, leading to a telehealth platform where
  patients aren't properly informed about potential treatment complications, resulting in
  several adverse events that might have been avoided with more complete information.

**Criterion question (answer this for the benchmark under review):**
> Do you use human evaluators for assessing system performance across all outputs
> evaluated by computational means when the outputs are evaluated as unacceptable?

## Mitigation-specific glossary (adds to the shared glossary)

- **Correctness:** The property of a benchmark being free from significant errors that
  could mislead or bias outcomes. It asks, "could the benchmark results be systematically
  wrong (e.g., biased) in some way?"
- **Evaluator:** A tool, algorithm, model, framework, or human checking the output of the
  System Under Test (SUT) for correctness, safety, or some other measurable property.
- **Failing System Under Test (SUT) Outputs:** The incorrect, unreliable, noncompliant,
  unsafe, or otherwise wrong responses generated by a SUT during benchmarking (e.g.,
  hallucinations, policy violations, harmful outputs, non-compliance with task
  instructions). Here, the concern is outputs computationally flagged as unacceptable.

## What counts as evidence the mitigation is in place

The evidence indicates the developer would likely assert the mitigation is in place when
the documents show **all** of the following for the *currently published* benchmark.
Each id below is a `requirement` key in the output.

1. **`human_review_of_rejections`** — Every output that a computational evaluator scores as
   unacceptable/failing is subsequently reviewed by a human evaluator.
2. **`full_coverage_of_rejections`** — This human review covers all such
   computationally-rejected outputs (not just a sample), so false rejections are caught.

**Strong positive signals:** a stated policy that all auto-flagged "fail/unacceptable"
outputs are human-adjudicated; described human review step for rejected items; reported
overturn rate of automated rejections by humans; a two-stage pipeline where computational
screening is confirmed by humans.

**Negative / disqualifying signals:** automated rejections accepted as final with no human
check; only a sample of rejected outputs reviewed; human review only for accepted (not
rejected) outputs; human confirmation of rejections described only as a future plan.
