## The mitigation under review

- **Mitigation number:** 138
- **Mitigates failure mode:** #32 — *"Evaluator(s) have a high false reject rate
  (acceptable outputs rated as unacceptable)"*
- **Affected reliability dimension:** Correctness
- **Benchmark production stage:** (4) Output Evaluation
- **Risk reduction if adopted:** ~80% reduction in failure-mode likelihood; ~45.83%
  reduction in severity (context only — do not let these numbers change your verdict).
- **Example realization of the failure mode:** A benchmark uses human evaluators who are
  instructed to flag any potential safety concerns in SUT responses to medical questions.
  The evaluators, anxious about missing harmful content and lacking medical expertise,
  excessively reject valid medical information that mentions side effects or treatment
  risks, even when accurately presented with appropriate context. As a result, systems
  that provide thorough, nuanced medical information score poorly compared to those
  giving oversimplified but incomplete advice. The benchmark user selects a SUT that
  omits important risk information, leading to a telehealth platform where patients
  aren't properly informed about treatment complications, resulting in several adverse
  events that might have been avoided with more complete information.

**Criterion question (answer this for the benchmark under review):**
> Is the evaluator observed to reliably achieve a low false reject rate (e.g., compliant
> labeled as non-compliance)?

## Mitigation-specific glossary (adds to the shared glossary)

- **Correctness:** The property of a benchmark being free from significant errors that
  could mislead or bias outcomes. It asks, "could the benchmark results be systematically
  wrong (e.g., biased) in some way?"
- **Evaluator:** A tool, algorithm, model, framework, or human checking the output of the
  System Under Test (SUT) for correctness, safety, or some other measurable property.
- **Failing System Under Test (SUT) Outputs:** The incorrect, unreliable, noncompliant,
  unsafe, or otherwise wrong responses generated by a SUT during benchmarking (e.g.,
  hallucinations, policy violations, harmful outputs). Distinguishing these from
  acceptable outputs is what the evaluator must do correctly.

## What counts as evidence the mitigation is in place

The evidence indicates the developer would likely assert the mitigation is in place when
the documents show **all** of the following for the *currently published* benchmark.
Each id below is a `requirement` key in the output.

1. **`false_reject_measured`** — The evaluator's false reject rate (acceptable / compliant
   outputs incorrectly labeled unacceptable / non-compliant) is actually measured against
   a trusted reference, not merely assumed.
2. **`false_reject_low`** — The observed false reject rate is reliably low — the evidence
   shows the evaluator rarely flags compliant outputs as non-compliant.

**Strong positive signals:** a reported false reject / false positive rate (or
specificity) against a gold-standard set; a confusion matrix or error-rate breakdown for
the evaluator; validation showing compliant outputs are seldom rejected; a stated
threshold the evaluator meets for false rejections.

**Negative / disqualifying signals:** the evaluator is used with no measurement of its
false reject behavior; only overall accuracy reported with no breakdown of false
rejections; reported false reject rate is high; validation described as planned;
evaluator reliability asserted without an error-rate study against a reference.
