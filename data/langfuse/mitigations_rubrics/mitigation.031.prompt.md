## The mitigation under review

- **Mitigation number:** 31
- **Mitigates failure mode:** #28 — *"Evaluator tuned to SUT failing outputs and cannot
  generalize to new SUTs."*
- **Affected reliability dimension:** Correctness
- **Benchmark production stage:** (4) Output Evaluation
- **Risk reduction if adopted:** ~76.67% reduction in failure-mode likelihood; ~16.67%
  reduction in severity (context only — do not let these numbers change your verdict).
- **Example realization of the failure mode:** The benchmark team fine-tunes an automated
  evaluator using outputs from a specific SUT—say, an earlier version of a proprietary
  model like GPT-3.5—that exhibits particular failure modes such as verbosity,
  evasiveness, or specific phrasing patterns. The evaluator learns to detect and penalize
  these patterns, mistaking them for general quality issues. When a new, structurally
  different SUT is evaluated—such as a model trained with more concise or stylistically
  different outputs—the evaluator misjudges its performance, either unfairly penalizing it
  or awarding inflated scores. The benchmark user, relying on evaluator scores, selects a
  model that aligns with legacy failure patterns rather than actual quality or safety in
  novel systems.

**Criterion question (answer this for the benchmark under review):**
> Do you statistically confirm performance for each new system under test (SUT) by
> evaluating the benchmark's accuracy on a representative sample of that SUT's outputs?

## Mitigation-specific glossary (adds to the shared glossary)

- **Correctness:** The property of a benchmark being free from significant errors that
  could mislead or bias outcomes. It asks, "could the benchmark results be systematically
  wrong (e.g., biased) in some way?"
- **Evaluator:** A tool, algorithm, model, framework, or human checking the output of the
  System Under Test (SUT) for correctness, safety, or some other measurable property.
- **Failing System Under Test (SUT) Outputs:** The incorrect, unreliable, noncompliant,
  unsafe, or otherwise wrong responses generated by a SUT during benchmarking (e.g.,
  hallucinations, policy violations, harmful outputs, non-compliance with task
  instructions).

## What counts as evidence the mitigation is in place

The evidence indicates the developer would likely assert the mitigation is in place when
the documents show **all** of the following for the *currently published* benchmark.
Each id below is a `requirement` key in the output.

1. **`per_sut_confirmation`** — Evaluator performance is confirmed for **each** new SUT,
   rather than validated once against a single reference SUT and assumed to generalize.
2. **`representative_sample`** — That confirmation is done on a **representative sample**
   of the new SUT's own outputs.
3. **`statistical_accuracy`** — The confirmation is statistical — the evaluator's accuracy
   (e.g., agreement with human/gold labels) on that SUT's outputs is measured and
   reported.

**Strong positive signals:** a reported evaluator-accuracy or human-agreement check run
per SUT before trusting its scores; a representative output sample drawn from each new SUT
for validation; statistics (accuracy, agreement, error rate) of the evaluator on the new
SUT's outputs; a stated protocol requiring per-SUT evaluator calibration.

**Negative / disqualifying signals:** the evaluator validated only against the SUT it was
tuned on; no per-SUT accuracy confirmation; assumption that the evaluator generalizes to
new systems without checking; validation on a non-representative or tiny sample; per-SUT
confirmation promised but not performed for the published results.
