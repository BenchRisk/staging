## The mitigation under review

- **Mitigation number:** 186
- **Mitigates failure mode:** #1 — *"The information provided by the benchmark does not
  match with the information the benchmark user believes is provided."*
- **Affected reliability dimension:** Intelligibility
- **Benchmark production stage:** (1) Task Definition
- **Risk reduction if adopted:** ~70.0% reduction in failure-mode likelihood; ~25.0%
  reduction in severity (context only — do not let these numbers change your verdict).
- **Example realization of the failure mode:** The benchmark presents a safety score for
  a SUT that does medical prescription dose recommendations, and the user believes
  "safety" includes dose safety, but the safety benchmark only tests whether the model
  will help someone commit violent acts; the user gets a stomach ulcer from consuming too
  many NSAIDs.

**Criterion question (answer this for the benchmark under review):**
> Do you provide a task taxonomy that shows how the input/output space maps to hazards,
> failures, or compliant/non-compliant prompts and outputs?

## Mitigation-specific glossary (adds to the shared glossary)

- **Intelligibility:** The clarity and ease with which benchmark results can be
  understood by intended users, ensuring they can accurately interpret and use the
  benchmark for real-world decisions. It asks, "will the relying user understand the LLM
  properties as evidenced by the benchmark?"
- **Task Definition:** The explicit and detailed specification of a SUT Task's
  parameters, requirements, and expectations, minimizing ambiguities that could lead to
  inconsistencies or errors.
- **Failing System Under Test (SUT) Outputs:** The incorrect, unreliable, noncompliant,
  unsafe, or otherwise wrong responses generated by a SUT during benchmarking (e.g.,
  hallucinations, policy violations, harmful outputs, bias, misinformation).
- **Normative Properties:** Characteristics, standards, or criteria that define what is
  considered acceptable, desirable, or expected within a context or domain, guiding how
  systems or outputs are assessed (e.g., which prompts/outputs count as compliant).

## What counts as evidence the mitigation is in place

The evidence indicates the developer would likely assert the mitigation is in place when
the documents show **all** of the following for the *currently published* benchmark.
Each id below is a `requirement` key in the output.

1. **`taxonomy_provided`** — The benchmark provides a task taxonomy (a structured
   breakdown of the input/output space), not merely an unstructured description.
2. **`maps_to_hazards_or_compliance`** — That taxonomy explicitly maps the input/output
   space to hazards, failures, or compliant/non-compliant prompts and outputs, so a user
   sees what the score does and does not cover.

**Strong positive signals:** a named taxonomy, category tree, or hazard/harm taxonomy
with defined classes; a table or figure mapping input/output categories to hazards or to
compliant vs. non-compliant labels; coverage of which prompt/output types are in scope;
clear delineation of what counts as a failure for each category.

**Negative / disqualifying signals:** only a high-level label (e.g., "safety") with no
breakdown of categories; no mapping from inputs/outputs to hazards or compliance; the
taxonomy must be inferred by the reader; a taxonomy described only for a future release
while the published benchmark omits it.
